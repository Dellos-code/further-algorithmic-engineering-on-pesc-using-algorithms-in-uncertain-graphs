\begin{abstract}
\noindent W1 is a deterministic, training-free heuristic that ranks the vertices of an uncertain graph by their expected reachable mass. It runs a normalised diffusion with the calibrated escape-probability damping rule to its fixed point, polishes the result with three raw propagation steps on a second operator, and fuses the ranks of the two phases. This report documents how W1 was chosen and how it performs. It was selected among \V{s2/configs} configurations of a staged search on a development panel of \V{dev/count} graphs, frozen with a recorded SHA-256 digest, and then evaluated on seven held-out panels with graphs of up to $10{,}000$ vertices, on which it was never tuned. Its mean Spearman correlation with the ground truth is the highest of all deployable comparators on six of the seven panels; on the panel of the largest graphs, two-iteration message passing has a higher mean, without a significant difference. The differences to IRIE and to the calibrated damping rules are significant after Holm correction on both primary panels. W1 also matches or exceeds the per-instance best constant damping, an upper bound of the constant-damping family, on graphs of up to about 150 vertices, but falls below it on graphs of 900 vertices and more; the loss is concentrated in the regime of high edge probabilities, and taking more propagation steps does not remove it. On the 100-instance hold-out of the earlier escape-probability study it ranks second of \V{ext/methods} methods, after Monte Carlo with 200 worlds. Every number of this report is regenerated by the scripts of the accompanying repository, which reproduce the stored results of the source study to within $10^{-16}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $G=(V,E)$ be an undirected graph whose edges are present independently with probabilities $p_{ij}$, and let $x>0$, $\sum_ix_i=1$, be a prior over the vertices. The expected reachable mass of vertex $i$ is $y^*_i=\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{v\in C(i)}x_v\big]$, where $C(i)$ is the connected component of $i$ in a sampled world; for uniform $x$ it is the single-seed influence spread of the independent cascade model. The task is to rank the vertices by $y^*$.

W1 belongs to the dual-operator polishing family introduced in the accompanying paper. Its specification is given in a separate document (\path{docs/specification/W1_specification_en.pdf}); in brief, Phase~A runs the lazy normalised diffusion on the escape-scaled pull operator $EW=\mathrm{diag}(\Pesc)W$ with the calibrated scalar damping $\alpha_A=\mathrm{clip}(0.502+0.558\,\Pescbar,0.4,1)$ of the earlier escape-probability study to its fixed point; its output is embedded in the excess scale with $\gamma=16$; Phase~B runs $K=3$ raw steps $b\leftarrow0.3x+0.7\,EP\,b$ with $EP=\mathrm{diag}(\Pesc)P$; and the output is the rank fusion $0.3\,r(a)+0.7\,r(b)$. The configuration of the paper itself, Phase~B on $P$ with damping $0.9$, five steps, $\gamma=1$ and equal weights, is reported as CTRL.

This report answers three questions. How was W1 chosen, and could the choice have been influenced by the data on which it is evaluated (Section~\ref{sec:selection})? How does it compare with the alternatives on data it has never seen (Sections~\ref{sec:confirmation} and~\ref{sec:external})? And where are its limits (Sections~\ref{sec:exploratory} and~\ref{sec:deviations})? Section~\ref{sec:reproduction} describes how every result is regenerated.

\subsection{Ground truth and metrics}

Every instance carries two independent Monte-Carlo estimates of $y^*$ over $2{,}000$ sampled worlds each: $y^A$ for evaluation and $y^B$ for fitting the oracles. The primary metric is the Spearman correlation $\rho$ between a method's scores and $y^A$, with scores equal to within a relative $10^{-12}$ treated as ties; Kendall's $\tau_b$, the overlap of the top 10\% and NDCG at 10\% are secondary. The ceiling column of Table~\ref{tab:panels} shows that the reference is itself noisy: two independent estimates agree to a mean Spearman correlation between $0.95$ and $0.99$. A method that ranked by the exact $y^*$ would therefore score somewhat below 1 against $y^A$, and a method can exceed the ceiling, which compares two noisy estimates with each other. Since all methods are scored against the same $y^A$, this noise is shared by the paired comparisons below.

\subsection{Comparators}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item[IRIE] influence ranking by iterative estimation (Jung, Heo and Chen, ICDM 2012): $y=x+a\,Py$ with $a=\min(0.7,0.95/\rho(P))$.
\item[EW rule] the escape-scaled pull diffusion with the calibrated damping rule of the earlier study; this is Phase~A of W1 on its own.
\item[WT rule] personalised PageRank on the push operator $W^{\top}$, with its own calibrated rule
\[\alpha=\mathrm{clip}(0.241+0.859\,\Pescbar,\,0.4,\,1).\]
\item[MP2] two iterations of the message passing of Karrer, Newman and Zdeborová.
\item[$\alpha{=}0.85$] the escape-scaled pull diffusion with the conventional damping $0.85$.
\item[oracle] for each instance, the constant damping of the escape-scaled pull diffusion (grid of 40 values) whose ranking agrees best with $y^B$. It uses ground-truth information and cannot be deployed; it is an upper bound of what any rule that predicts one constant damping per graph can achieve.
\item[CTRL] the dual-operator configuration of the accompanying paper (added after the fact, Section~\ref{sec:ctrl}).
\end{description}

\subsection{Statistical procedure}

For each held-out panel, the frozen confirmatory family consists of the twelve contrasts \{W1, W3\} $\times$ \{IRIE, EW rule, WT rule, MP2, $\alpha{=}0.85$, oracle\}. Each contrast is a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the per-instance differences of $\rho$, with exact zero differences dropped; the twelve $p$-values of a panel are adjusted by Holm's method. The 95\% intervals of the mean differences are percentile bootstrap intervals with $20{,}000$ instance-level resamples. W3 is the second frozen configuration; its contrasts are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:w3}.

\section{Selection on the development panel}\label{sec:selection}

\subsection{Search}

The search evaluated configurations of the form ``Phase~A, excess embedding with $\gamma$, $K$ raw Phase-B steps on an operator with a damping law, rank fusion with weight $\beta$'' on all \V{dev/count} development instances, in four stages.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item[S0] an audit reproducing the published values of CTRL ($\bar\rho=\V{dev/CTRL}$), the EW rule, the WT rule and IRIE.
\item[Comparators] 19 methods, among them the oracle ($\V{dev/oracle}$), IRIE ($\V{dev/IRIE}$) and the EW rule ($\V{dev/ruleEW}$).
\item[S2] a full grid of \V{s2/configs} configurations: six Phase-B operators ($P$, $EP$, the log-weight matrix $P_{\log}$, the row-normalised $P$, $W$, $EW$), 20 damping laws (14 constants from $0.3$ to $1$ and six multiples of $1/\rho$ of the operator), 14 step counts from 1 to 50, $\beta\in\{0.3,\dots,0.7\}$ and $\gamma\in\{1,4\}$. Best: $\V{s2/best}$.
\item[S3] a refinement of the 50 best S2 configurations over $\beta\in\{0,0.05,\dots,1\}$ and $\gamma\in\{0.25,\dots,16\}$, together with nine variable damping laws (the two calibrated rules, the per-node law $\alpha_i=gS_i/(x_i+gS_i)$ with $S=Ty$, its mean-field version, and the anchored proportional law of the paper): \V{s3/rows} evaluations per instance, of which \V{s3/configs} are distinct configurations. Best: $\V{dev/W1}$.
\item[S4] two alternating schedules of Phase~A and Phase~B blocks; best $\V{dev/S4best}$.
\end{description}

\subsection{What the search found}

Table~\ref{tab:top} lists the ten best configurations. The optimum is a plateau rather than a peak: \V{plateau/0.001} configurations lie within $0.001$ of the best, \V{plateau/0.002} within $0.002$ and \V{plateau/0.005} within $0.005$. All configurations within $0.002$ take exactly three Phase-B steps on one of the raw operators $EP$, $P$ or $P_{\log}$, with a damping between $0.5$ and $0.8$ and $\beta$ between $0.6$ and $0.85$. The step count is the most consequential parameter (Table~\ref{tab:steps}): the best S2 mean rises from one to three steps and decreases monotonically beyond. Phase~B on a normalised operator ($W$, $EW$, row-normalised $P$) is markedly worse (best $\V{s2op/W}$ to $\V{s2op/EW}$, against $\V{s2op/Plog}$ for $P_{\log}$).

No variable damping law reached the plateau. At $\gamma=4$, the value at which the laws were examined, the best constant damping reaches $\V{law/const4}$; the best per-node law reaches $\V{law/D2}$, its mean-field version $\V{law/D3}$ and the anchored proportional law $\V{law/D4}$. The search therefore confirms, from the side of the polishing step, the conclusion of the paper that per-node proportional damping does not improve on a scalar damping.

\subsection{Freeze}

The selection file \path{selected_mixing.json} was written at \V{freeze/utc} UTC. It names W1, with development mean $\V{dev/W1}$, and W3, with $\V{dev/W3}$, identical to W1 except for $\beta=0.75$. The best alternating schedule ($\V{dev/S4best}$) did not exceed W1, so no schedule was frozen.

The development data cannot distinguish W1 from its neighbours on the plateau. In $2{,}000$ instance-level bootstrap resamples of the development panel, W1 has the highest mean in a fraction $\V{boot/W1}$ of the resamples and W3 in $\V{boot/W3}$, while \V{boot/ever} different configurations are best at least once (the source study reported $\V{boot/W1src}$ and $\V{boot/W3src}$ with a different seed). The confirmation results below should therefore be read as results for the plateau as much as for W1 itself.

\section{Exploratory analyses}\label{sec:exploratory}

The analyses of this section were not preregistered and use the confirmation panels after the fact. They describe the frozen W1; none of them was used to change it.

\subsection{Cost against Monte Carlo}

Monte-Carlo estimation with $k$ sampled worlds is the direct alternative to any heuristic. Table~\ref{tab:cost} compares W1 with Monte Carlo on identical instances, single-threaded on one machine (W1 timed end to end, operator construction included; each timing the minimum of three repetitions, one on graphs above 1,000 vertices). On graphs of 100 vertices (\p{test2}), Monte Carlo with 200 worlds is faster than W1 (median $\V{cost/test2/MC200ms}$ against $\V{cost/test2/W1ms}$ ms) and about as accurate (W1 $-$ MC-200: $\V{cost/test2/vsMC200/d}$, wins/losses \V{cost/test2/vsMC200/wl}, $p=\V{cost/test2/vsMC200/p}$), and Monte Carlo with 500 worlds is more accurate ($\V{cost/test2/vsMC500/d}$, $p=\V{cost/test2/vsMC500/p}$) at $\V{cost/test2/ratio500}$ times the time of W1. On graphs of 900 vertices (\p{scale2}), W1 is $\V{cost/scale2/ratio200}$ times faster than 200 worlds, with a similar accuracy ($\V{cost/scale2/vsMC200/d}$, \V{cost/scale2/vsMC200/wl}, $p=\V{cost/scale2/vsMC200/p}$), and 500 worlds are significantly more accurate ($\V{cost/scale2/vsMC500/d}$, $p=\V{cost/scale2/vsMC500/p}$) at $\V{cost/scale2/ratio500}$ times its time. \subsection{Sensitivity to the two normalisation conventions}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} varies the two scale conventions of the specification. Dividing the Phase-B starting point by $1+\gamma$ lowers $\rho$ on every panel: the recursion of Phase~B is affine, so the scale of its starting point relative to $x$ is a parameter, and the excess-scale convention fixes it. Without the per-step normalisation, Phase~A converges to the resolvent $(1-\alpha_A)(I-\alpha_AEW)^{-1}x$ instead of the Perron vector of the specification, which equals the resolvent with the larger effective damping $\alpha_A/\lambda$ ($\lambda$ the Perron root); when $\alpha_A=1$ (\V{ab/alpha1all} of \V{ab/instances} instances) its mass decays geometrically, so that the stopping rule is met because the iterate becomes small rather than because it has stabilised. Phase~A on its own is worse without the normalisation on every panel. The effect on W1 is mixed: removing the normalisation lowers $\rho$ on \p{test2}, \p{prior2} and \p{real2}, leaves it unchanged on \p{scale2} and raises it on \p{big}. The gain is confined to the high-probability regimes, where $\alpha_A$ is at or near 1 and the normalised Phase~A approaches the Perron vector of $EW$ alone (\p{big}, R4: mean $\alpha_A=\V{ab/big/R4/alpha}$, W1 $\V{ab/big/R4/W1}$, without normalisation $\V{ab/big/R4/unnorm}$); in the other regimes the normalisation helps. The calibrated rule was fitted with the normalised iteration, so this does not suggest removing it; it marks the behaviour of Phase~A at $\alpha_A\approx1$ as the place where W1 can be improved.

At the lowest edge probabilities (R1) the leading methods are close to the noise ceiling and W1 is marginally below IRIE, the EW rule and the oracle (\p{test2}: W1 $\V{reg/test2/R1/W1}$, IRIE $\V{reg/test2/R1/IRIE}$, oracle $\V{reg/test2/R1/oracle}$). At high probabilities, however, two-iteration message passing is the strongest deployable method (\p{test2}, R5: MP2 $\V{reg/test2/R5/MP2}$, W1 $\V{reg/test2/R5/W1}$; \p{big}, R4: MP2 $\V{reg/big/R4/MP2}$, W1 $\V{reg/big/R4/W1}$), and on large graphs the oracle is far ahead (\p{big}, R4: $\V{reg/big/R4/oracle}$). The loss of W1 against the oracle on large graphs is therefore a loss in the supercritical regime, in which a giant component forms in most worlds and the calibrated rule sets $\alpha_A$ at or near 1; at low probabilities W1 exceeds the oracle on \p{big} (R2: $\V{reg/big/R2/W1}$ against $\V{reg/big/R2/oracle}$). A selector that hands the high-probability regime to message passing is a natural next step; it was not part of the search.

\section{Protocol deviations and limitations}\label{sec:deviations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Tie-break of W1.} The declared rule was: among configurations within $0.001$ of the best, prefer fewer free parameters, then lower measured cost. All \V{plateau/0.001} candidates have the same parameter set, and the freeze took the configuration with the highest mean. Applied to the stored cost estimates, the cost criterion would have chosen the configuration ranked \V{tie/altrank} (operator $P_{\log}$, damping \V{tie/altdamp}, $\beta=\V{tie/altbeta}$; development mean $\V{tie/altdev}$), whose median cost ($\V{tie/mincost}$ ms) differs from that of W1 ($\V{tie/W1cost}$ ms) by less than the run-to-run variation of such timings.
\item \textbf{Rule of W3.} W3 was declared as the configuration with the fewest free parameters within $0.002$ of W1; since all candidates have the same parameter set, the rule does not single out a simpler configuration, and the frozen W3 is the second-ranked configuration.
\item \textbf{Bootstrap.} The source study did not commit the code of its bootstrap of the top-1 frequencies; they are recomputed here with this repository's seed.
\item \textbf{Comparator family.} The declared family named a ``best constant damping on the development panel''; the stored confirmation runs used the conventional $\alpha=0.85$ and the per-instance oracle instead. CTRL, also declared, was missing from the stored runs and is reported here separately (Section~\ref{sec:ctrl}).
\item \textbf{Panel \p{real2}.} Only the 24 instances built on three classic networks exist; the snowball samples of four larger networks in the original specification could not be built, because the source graphs were unavailable. Conclusions about real networks rest on few, small graphs.
\item \textbf{Labels of the source statistics files.} The columns \texttt{t} and \texttt{l} of the source study's statistics files contain losses and ties; the reproduced files name them accordingly.
\item \textbf{Boundaries of the search.} $\gamma=16$ is the largest value of its grid. On the development panel, larger values give $\V{ab/dev/gamma32}$ ($\gamma=32$), $\V{ab/dev/gamma64}$ ($64$) and $\V{ab/dev/gamma128}$ ($128$), against $\V{ab/dev/gamma16}$ at $\gamma=16$, so the optimum lies inside the extended range (exploratory). The step count and every other parameter were selected on graphs of 100 vertices; Section~\ref{sec:ksweep} shows that $K=3$ remains the best choice on larger graphs.
\item \textbf{Spectral radius.} The damping family ``$c/\rho(T)$'' of stage S2 computed $\rho(T)$ with a symmetric eigensolver also for non-symmetric operators. The frozen configurations do not use this family; it is kept unchanged for reproducibility.
\item \textbf{Scope.} All graphs have at most 10,000 vertices, edges are independent, the ground truth is itself a Monte-Carlo estimate, and timings are of a Python implementation on one machine.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproduction}\label{sec:reproduction}

The repository contains the code, the data and the stored results of the source study (\path{evidence/original}), and regenerates every result in \path{evidence/reproduced}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
\item all \V{repro/instances} instances are rebuilt from their specifications and are identical bit for bit (\path{scripts/verify_data.py}); \V{repro/gt} ground-truth samples were recomputed and are identical;
\item the digest of the frozen selection matches, W1 is the best development configuration of the stored search, and the frozen development means are recovered exactly (\path{scripts/verify_freeze.py});
\item \V{repro/searchevals} re-evaluations of \V{repro/searchcfgs} search configurations on the development panel agree with the stored values to within $\V{repro/searchmax}$ (\path{scripts/run_search.py});
\item the \V{repro/confirmrows} stored confirmation rows, four metrics each, are reproduced to within $\V{repro/confirmmax}$ (\path{scripts/run_confirm.py}), and the stored statistics to within $\V{repro/statsmax}$ (\path{scripts/analyze.py});
\item the external hold-out values of W1 and W3 are reproduced exactly (\path{scripts/run_external_holdout.py}).
\end{itemize}
The script \path{reproduce.sh} runs every step; \path{scripts/make_doc_assets.py} writes every number of this report.
